# Content Strategy

## 1. Document Purpose

### 1.1 Objective
Define the content, tone, proof, media, and client-supplied assets required to make the Luxury Hotel Website Demo persuasive, accurate, and maintainable.

### 1.2 Content Principle
The site should use **specific truth with emotional presentation**. It must never use vague luxury language to compensate for missing facts.

## 2. Voice and Tone

### 2.1 Voice Attributes
Copy should feel:

- calm;
- warm;
- confident;
- concise;
- sensory without exaggeration;
- locally grounded;
- welcoming;
- practical when the guest needs facts.

### 2.2 Writing Style
Use short paragraphs, strong headings, and concrete details. Avoid long blocks of brochure copy.

### 2.3 Words and Phrases to Avoid
Avoid unsupported or overused claims such as:

- world-class;
- unparalleled;
- ultimate luxury;
- perfect;
- flawless;
- best hotel in Kandy;
- five-star experience;
- once-in-a-lifetime.

Use such language only when objectively supportable and approved.

## 3. Messaging Hierarchy

### 3.1 Primary Message
The property is a peaceful scenic stay near Kandy with strong nature, view, hospitality, and dining potential.

### 3.2 Secondary Messages
Support the primary message with:

- room comfort;
- breakfast/dining;
- direct contact;
- Kandy exploration;
- verified guest sentiment;
- practical travel information.

### 3.3 Conversion Message
Make direct enquiry feel easy and personal:

**Check your dates, message the property, or call directly.**

## 4. Homepage Content

### 4.1 Hero
Required:

- property name;
- one emotional headline;
- one short supporting statement;
- primary CTA;
- secondary CTA;
- strongest hero visual.

### 4.2 Property Story
Use approximately 60-120 words explaining the setting and stay character.

### 4.3 Featured Rooms
For each room:

- official name;
- one-line positioning;
- size;
- occupancy;
- bed;
- view;
- 2-4 verified feature tags;
- primary image.

### 4.4 View / Nature Section
Use only verified environmental qualities and real photography.

### 4.5 Experiences Preview
Feature only experiences the property can genuinely arrange or destination experiences described as independent Kandy activities.

### 4.6 Dining Preview
Required where confirmed:

- dining identity;
- breakfast information;
- cuisine;
- dietary options;
- service hours;
- one strong food/restaurant visual.

### 4.7 Guest Proof
Use:

- current rating;
- source;
- current review count;
- short attributed review excerpts;
- maintenance date for rating data.

### 4.8 Kandy Preview
Explain why the property works as a quieter base for exploring Kandy.

### 4.9 Final Conversion
Use a concise invitation and the availability/WhatsApp options.

## 5. Stay Page Content

### 5.1 Page Intro
Explain the accommodation collection in under 100 words.

### 5.2 Room Data Requirements
Every active room should have:

- official current name;
- slug;
- short description;
- full description;
- size;
- max adults;
- max children if applicable;
- bed configuration;
- view;
- balcony/terrace;
- bathroom facts;
- air conditioning;
- amenity list;
- inclusions;
- restrictions;
- photo set.

### 5.3 Missing Data Rule
If a field is not verified, omit it rather than guessing.

## 6. Room Detail Content

### 6.1 Opening Copy
Use approximately 40-90 words focused on what makes the room distinct.

### 6.2 Practical Facts
Facts must be scan-friendly and consistent across rooms.

### 6.3 Amenity Groups
Recommended groups:

- Sleep
- Bathroom
- View and Outdoor
- Comfort
- Connectivity
- Services

### 6.4 Policies
Include room-specific rules only when confirmed.

### 6.5 Related Rooms
Use real differences, not arbitrary “recommended” labels.

## 7. Experiences Content

### 7.1 Required Fields
For each experience:

- name;
- category;
- short summary;
- full description if needed;
- duration if known;
- availability;
- who operates it;
- what is included;
- what guests should know;
- enquiry method;
- imagery.

### 7.2 Operator Clarity
If an activity is supplied by a third party, state that clearly.

## 8. Dining Content

### 8.1 Restaurant Information
Request:

- official name;
- cuisine;
- opening hours;
- meal periods;
- reservation requirement;
- indoor/outdoor setting;
- dietary support;
- menus;
- dress guidance if any.

### 8.2 Breakfast
Breakfast deserves dedicated content if guest sentiment supports it. Confirm:

- included or paid;
- service times;
- available styles;
- dietary options.

### 8.3 Food Photography
Prefer real dishes and dining spaces. Avoid unrelated stock food photography.

## 9. Gallery Content

### 9.1 Required Categories
Target a balanced set across:

- exterior/property;
- rooms;
- bathrooms;
- balconies;
- views;
- dining;
- breakfast;
- people/service;
- evening atmosphere;
- Kandy/destination.

### 9.2 Minimum Useful Set
A premium demo should target at least 25-40 strong, non-repetitive images if the client can supply them.

### 9.3 Image Rights
Every production image must be client-owned, licensed, or explicitly authorised.

## 10. Kandy Content

### 10.1 Role
The Kandy page should help guests decide whether the location works for their trip.

### 10.2 Required Destination Data
For each selected attraction:

- official name;
- short description;
- approximate distance or travel-time wording;
- source/verification note;
- image rights;
- map link.

### 10.3 Travel-Time Disclaimer
Avoid absolute timing promises because traffic and road conditions vary.

## 11. Offers Content

### 11.1 Required Fields
Every offer needs:

- name;
- audience;
- benefit;
- booking window;
- stay window;
- inclusions;
- exclusions;
- conditions;
- CTA;
- image.

### 11.2 Offer Approval
Offers must be explicitly approved by the property before publication.

## 12. FAQ Content

### 12.1 Initial Questions
Collect verified answers for:

- check-in and check-out;
- Wi-Fi;
- parking;
- breakfast;
- dining;
- airport transfer;
- distance to Kandy;
- children;
- extra beds;
- pets;
- smoking;
- payment;
- cancellation;
- accessibility;
- direct booking;
- late arrival.

### 12.2 Plain-Language Rule
Answers should be direct and practical, not promotional.

## 13. Contact Content

### 13.1 Required Business Information
Collect:

- legal/operating property name;
- full address;
- primary phone;
- WhatsApp number;
- reservation email;
- general email if different;
- map location;
- social profiles;
- reception/contact hours if applicable.

## 14. Reviews and Ratings

### 14.1 Approved Sources
Use reputable current sources such as Google and major travel platforms.

### 14.2 Maintenance
Store:

- rating value;
- review count;
- source;
- source URL;
- date checked.

### 14.3 Excerpt Rule
Use short excerpts only and preserve attribution. Do not combine multiple reviews into a synthetic testimonial.

## 15. SEO Content

### 15.1 Page Titles
Write unique titles for each route.

### 15.2 Meta Descriptions
Use accurate 140-165 character working targets without keyword stuffing.

### 15.3 Heading Content
Use one clear H1 per page and descriptive H2/H3 hierarchy.

### 15.4 Search Themes
Natural search themes may include:

- Kandy hotel;
- scenic stay near Kandy;
- nature stay near Kandy;
- Kandy accommodation;
- Kandy resort;
- rooms near Kandy.

Final keyword strategy should be based on actual search data if SEO becomes a paid deliverable.

## 16. CMS Content Model

### 16.1 Site Settings
Store:

- property name;
- contact;
- social;
- booking URL;
- WhatsApp;
- default SEO;
- current ratings.

### 16.2 Core Documents
CMS should support:

- Room
- Experience
- Dining
- Offer
- FAQ
- Review
- Gallery Item
- Attraction
- Page/Editorial Section

### 16.3 Editorial Controls
Editors should be able to change content without changing layout structure.

## 17. Client Asset Request

### 17.1 First Request Package
Ask the client for:

- logo files;
- brand colours if any;
- official room list;
- room facts;
- property facilities;
- contact details;
- policies;
- dining information;
- offers;
- experiences;
- high-resolution photography;
- social links;
- preferred booking process.

### 17.2 Missing Asset Strategy
If the client lacks photography, quote photography separately rather than filling the final site with unlicensed third-party media.

## 18. Demo Content Rules

### 18.1 Internal Concept Content
Clearly label assumptions in internal files.

### 18.2 Public Demo
If the demo is public before client approval, avoid presenting speculative property claims as fact.

### 18.3 Client Review
Require a factual content approval pass before production launch.

## 19. Content Acceptance Criteria

### 19.1 Accuracy
Every property-specific factual claim is verified or removed.

### 19.2 Completeness
Core pages have enough content to look intentional and useful.

### 19.3 Consistency
Room facts use the same naming and units across pages.

### 19.4 Tone
Copy feels premium without exaggerated claims.

### 19.5 Rights
Production imagery and review excerpts have a defensible usage basis.
